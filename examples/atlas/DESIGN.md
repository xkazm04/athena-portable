# Design System: atlas — `plate`

Produced by following the `google-labs-code/stitch-skills` DESIGN.md discipline, in the 9-section
`taste-design` format (atmosphere, colour, type, components, layout, hero, responsive, motion,
banned) — the same format `examples/tidycrm/DESIGN.md` uses, and for the same reason: the
5-section `design-md` variant has no spacing scale, no motion section and no scales of any kind,
and could not describe an app whose whole subject is a machine drawn to scale.

**This file is law, not values.** The values live in `components/atlas/style/base/tokens.css`,
scoped to `[data-variant="plate"]` and imported first by `components/atlas/style/index.css`. Where
a number appears below it appears as a *rule about* a token; the token file is the authority and
nothing here re-declares a value it owns.

**One direction, no variant experiment.** Atlas is the formula's objective test (`docs/layered-ui-formula.md`
§2, round 2): an app built only through `@athena/demo-kit`'s primitives, whose count of
"I had to write this myself" is the distance from a reusable formula. A prototyping round with
three live directions would measure the directions instead. So: one direction, one render, one
`[data-variant]` block, `ThemeVariantSwitcher` not mounted.

---

## 0. Dials

`taste-design`'s four dials are the only mechanism in the reference that *generates* a direction
rather than describing one. `plate` is generated by them, and the settings are the brief:

| Dial | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Creativity | **2 / 5** | The subject is a repository's own architecture. A surface that editorialises about it is lying about how much interpretation went in. The invention budget is spent on one metaphor and nothing else. |
| Density | **4 / 5** | Sixty-eight components, eighteen systems, seventeen concepts and a hundred and twenty edges have to be *comparable*, which means on one plate at once. Density is the content, not a preference. |
| Variance | **1 / 5** | Every cell on a drawing plate is the same cell. A component row at L1 and the component row inside the L2 pane are one component drawn at two scales, never two designs. |
| Motion | **2 / 5** | One direction, no bounce, no spring, no idle. A level change is a followable move and everything else holds still. |

The occupied corners this avoids: `ledgerbox/lanes` is dark-teal and dense-editorial,
`hirelane/board` is dark-gold and photographic, `tidycrm/blocks` is light vellum and drafted.
`plate` takes the unoccupied one — **dark blue, and printed rather than drawn**.

---

## 1. Visual Theme & Atmosphere

### `plate` — "The Plate"

A **cyanotype**: an architect's general-arrangement drawing, contact-printed onto Prussian-blue
paper so the drawn line comes out white and the paper comes out blue. It is the oldest thing in
the world that *is* a map of a machine — you do not read a blueprint for how it looks, you read
it to find the part you need and what it connects to, which is exactly what Atlas is for.

What the metaphor buys, clause by clause:

- **The ground is the material, not a theme.** There is no "dark mode" here; a cyanotype is blue
  because of the chemistry. So there is one polarity, `--at-ground` through `--at-plate-3`, and no
  light variant is owed.
- **Depth is rule weight, not shadow.** `--at-elev-0: none`. Nothing on this surface casts a
  shadow, ever. A cell that is nearer is a cell drawn with a heavier rule (`--at-rule-2`, then
  `--at-rule-3` for the one thing currently open), and a cell that is further back is drawn in
  `--at-line-3`. Three weights, and they are the whole vocabulary of hierarchy.
- **Corners are square.** `--at-radius-0: 0px` on every box. A plotted line does not round.
- **The grid is visible.** A faint cyanotype grid (`--at-grid`, four spacing steps) sits under the
  plate at L0 and L1, in `--at-grid-ink`. It is what makes the surface read as a sheet with things
  arranged *on* it rather than as a page with panels.
- **Every entry cites its source.** A part on a drawing carries a part number; every concept,
  system and component in Atlas carries the repository path or the README/ADR section it was
  extracted from, set in the figure face. This is the app's one non-negotiable content rule: the
  model was extracted by hand, so the surface has to show its work.

The atmosphere is **still and lit**. Nothing pulses, nothing breathes, nothing implies work is
happening — Athena is not connected to this app, and a surface that animates at rest reads as one
that is thinking. The only thing that moves without being asked is nothing at all.

## 2. Colour Palette & Roles

Authority: `components/atlas/style/base/tokens.css`. Every colour is emitted as an `--at-*` custom
property scoped to `[data-variant="plate"]`, and the block ends with an alias layer re-pointing
the kit's `--dk-*` hooks at those values, so any kit primitive this app mounts — the toast
provider today, a badge or an activity chip tomorrow — follows the direction without knowing it
exists. **Alias, never fork.** (`AppShell` is deliberately not mounted: the mast is this app's
continuity, and a second header above it would be a second place to look for where you are.)

### The ramp

One neutral family, and it is blue. Four grounds and four inks, no warm/cool fluctuation inside
either — a cyanotype has exactly one pigment.

| Token | Role |
|---|---|
| `--at-ground` | the table the sheet lies on; the page behind everything |
| `--at-plate` | the sheet itself; the level container |
| `--at-plate-2` | a cell, a band, a pane — anything drawn *on* the sheet |
| `--at-plate-3` | a cell under the pointer, or the one cell that is open |
| `--at-line-3` | the faintest rule: the grid, a hairline between rows, a receded cell's border |
| `--at-line-2` | secondary rule and *non-essential* ink: counts a reader can skip, disabled chrome |
| `--at-line-1` | label ink — every small word on the sheet |
| `--at-line-0` | **the white line**: headings, the name of the thing, the drawn part |

`--at-line-2` is the lowest ink tier and is deliberately not allowed to carry a sentence anybody
has to follow. It clears AA against `--at-plate-2` (4.6:1) and does *not* clear it against
`--at-plate-3`, which is what a hovered cell is — so it is reserved for rule work, skimmable
figures and disabled chrome, and prose ink stops at `--at-line-1` (8.5:1). `--at-line-3` carries
no text at all. This is the same rule tidycrm arrived at for its graphite ramp (`DESIGN.md` §3)
and it is here because the mistake is the same one.

### The one accent

**Exactly one accent, `--at-mark` (vermilion), and it means exactly one thing: the lens.**

The lens is the app's whole argument. Choosing a concept at L0 — an invariant, a ladder tier, an
act of the demo — lights every system that touches it and every component that enforces it, all
the way down through `nav.highlight`. That is a claim about the repository ("invariant 3 is
enforced in these four places and nowhere else"), and a claim gets a hue. Nothing else on this
surface may take vermilion: not a hover, not a selection, not a count, not an error. If it is
orange, the lens put it there.

Vermilion on Prussian blue is the drafting convention this borrows from (the correction pencil on
the blue print), and it is the one hue with no neighbour anywhere in the ramp, so a single lit
component in a list of sixty is findable without search.

> **Divergence, declared.** `taste-design` §2 says never `#000000` — adopted, the ramp bottoms out
> at `--at-ground`, a blue near-black. It bans the "AI purple/blue neon" aesthetic — adopted in
> substance: there is no glow anywhere, no gradient fill, no chroma without a meaning, and the
> blue here is a *ground*, never a lit accent. A blue button that glows would break this file.

### Governance is a role, not a badge

Fixed across every app in `examples/` and not spendable on anything else:

| Role | Token | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| auto | `--dk-auto` | reversible; the agent may do this unattended |
| gated | `--dk-gated` | irreversible; a human arms it first |

Atlas registers **only reads and moves** — it has no database, writes nothing, and reaches
nobody — so every one of its tools is AUTO and `--dk-gated` appears nowhere in the running app.
It is still aliased (to a low-chroma ochre, distinguishable from `--at-mark` at a glance) because
the role must mean one thing across the studio whether or not this app spends it. A future tool
here that is not reversible finds the colour already waiting and correctly named.

## 3. Typography Rules

`taste-design` bans `Inter` outright and bans serif "in dashboards or software UIs regardless".
**Adopted: both.** A cyanotype has no written voice — nothing is hand-annotated on it — so the
serif exemption tidycrm argued for does not apply here, and there is no third face.

| Role | Face | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn (display, UI, prose) | **Chivo** | weight 400–900, self-hosted via `next/font` |
| Figures, paths, ids | **IBM Plex Mono** | weight 400/500, tabular by construction |

Both are loaded at the module scope of `app/layout.tsx` through `next/font/google`, which
downloads and self-hosts them at build time. **No runtime font request**, per repo law. Both
`--at-font-*` tokens name a real fallback stack, so a build with no font step renders the same
layout in system faces.

**Scale rules** (values as `--at-text-*`, `--at-leading-*`, `--at-track-*` in the token file):

- **One micro label, and it is not set in caps.** Every small word on the plate — a cell's kind, a
  band's layer name, a column head, the word under a figure — is one style: `--at-text-label`,
  sentence case, `--at-track-label`, `--at-line-1`. Tracked-out uppercase was tried and costs
  legibility on every word in the app in exchange for a drafting flavour the rules and the figure
  face already carry.
- **Every path, id, count and number is `--at-font-fig`**, tabular, at `--at-text-fig`. A column of
  file paths that does not align is not a drawing. This is unconditional; it is not behind the
  Density dial.
- **Display is weight-driven and track-tight**, never merely large. Leading compresses toward 1.05
  as size grows (`--at-leading-tight`); body sits at `--at-leading-body`.
- **Body never below 1rem.** `--at-text-base` *is* `1rem` and the root is left at the browser's
  own 16px. The reading sizes start there because a component's "what it enforces" sentence is
  read, not glanced at.
- **Prose is the only thing measure-clamped**, at `--at-measure` (62ch). Nothing else gets a
  max-width from type.
- **Fluid terms are on `cqi`, never `vw`.** An agent side panel narrows the body without changing
  the viewport, and a viewport-relative clamp would report a width this surface does not have.
  The level container is the container-query context.

## 4. Component Stylings

Five components carry the whole app. Every one of them is the same cell at a different scale
(Variance dial = 1).

### `Cell` — the atom

A square-cornered box on `--at-plate-2`, `--at-rule-1` in `--at-line-3`, `--at-space-3` of
padding. Three states and nothing else:

| State | Drawn as |
|---|---|
| at rest | `--at-rule-1` in `--at-line-3` |
| under the pointer / focused | ground to `--at-plate-3`, rule to `--at-line-2`, over `--at-dur-hair` |
| open (the one the reader is inside) | rule to `--at-rule-3` in `--at-line-0` |
| lit by the lens | rule to `--at-rule-2` in `--at-mark`, and a `--at-space-1` vermilion tick in the top-left corner |

"Lit" composes with the other three — the lens is orthogonal to where you are.

### `Part number`

Top-left of every cell, `--at-font-fig`, `--at-text-micro`, `--at-line-2`: `I-3`, `L-core`,
`C-041`. It is how a reader says a thing out loud and how a tool result cites it, and it is why
the ids in `data/` are short and stable.

### `Citation`

Bottom of every cell and pane: the repository path or the section the entry was extracted from,
`--at-font-fig`, `--at-text-micro`, `--at-line-2`, truncated from the *left* (`direction: rtl`
with an LTR span inside) so `…/core/catalog.py` survives and `src/athena/…` does not. Never a
link — nothing in this app fetches anything.

### `Band` — a layer of README §3.1

A full-width horizontal strip, `--at-space-6` tall, holding the layer's name at `--at-text-lg` on
the left, its systems' names as a row of labels in the middle, and its counts on the right. Six
of them stacked, in the order §3.1 prints them (surfaces at the top, contracts at the bottom),
with `--at-rule-1` hairlines between and no gap: a stack drawn as a stack. A band is the door to
L1 and is the shared element that becomes the L1 head.

### `Pane` — L2

The only element on the surface allowed to sit over the sheet. `--at-plate-2`, `--at-rule-3` in
`--at-line-0`, no shadow and no scrim blur — a scrim is a flat `--at-ground` at `--at-scrim-a`,
because frosted glass is not a thing that happens to paper.

## 5. Layout & Spacing

- **One spacing scale**, `--at-space-0` … `--at-space-8`, on a 4px grid, and the cyanotype grid
  under the plate is the same grid at 4×. Every margin, padding and gap in the app is one of
  these nine tokens. There is no raw `px` outside the token block — `design/check-tokens.mjs`
  fails lint on one.
- **The plate is a fixed spine, the content scrolls inside it.** The mast (app name, level rail,
  lens read-out) is `--at-space-7` tall and never moves between levels; it is the continuity the
  rubric's fourth axis asks for. Everything under it is the level.
- **L0 is two regions on one sheet**: the concept index (a `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(--at-cell-min, 1fr))`
  grid of concept cells) above, and the six-band layer stack below. They are on the same sheet and
  scroll together; the stack is not a sidebar.
- **L1 is a column set**, `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(--at-col-min, 1fr))`, one column per system in
  the open layer, each column a head plus its components as rows. Columns compare; that is the
  whole point of L1 and the reason systems are laid side by side rather than stacked.
- **Edges are drawn in the margin, not across the plate.** A system's edges to other layers are a
  left rail (layers above it) and a right rail (layers below it), each a short labelled tick. No
  bezier curves over the content: a line crossing forty component rows is decoration that makes
  the rows harder to read, and at this density a real node-link graph is a hairball. The graph is
  *navigable*, not *drawn*.
- **Nothing is centred except the pane.** The plate is read from the top-left like a drawing.

## 6. Hero — the three levels

The hero is not an image; it is the L0 plate, and it has to answer "what is this repository" in
one glance without a word of onboarding copy. Its argument, in order of eye travel:

1. **the index** — sixteen concepts, six of them invariants (drawn heavier), the rest the product
   ideas: four ladder tiers, four demo acts, and the standing decisions. This is what the
   repository *claims*.
2. **the stack** — six bands, top to bottom, surfaces → contracts. This is what it *is made of*.
3. **the lens** — click a concept and the stack marks up: every band shows how many of its
   components carry that concept, in vermilion. The claim and the machine, joined, without
   changing level.

L1 is "one layer, its systems laid out to compare, and what it connects to". L2 is "one
component: what it enforces, who calls it, which invariants it carries, and the ADR that explains
it". The reader should be able to get from "provenance is mandatory" to `Brain.write_fact` in two
clicks and see the ADR number on arrival.

## 7. Responsive

- The level container is the container-query context (`container-type: inline-size`); every fluid
  term is `cqi`.
- **≥ 1200cqi** — L0's index is a 4-up grid; L1 shows up to four system columns.
- **720–1199cqi** — index 2-up; L1 columns reflow to two, then one. A band's middle region (the
  system names) drops first; its name and counts never drop.
- **< 720cqi** — index 1-up, L1 single column, the margin edge-rails move under the column head as
  an inline list. The pane goes full-bleed with `--at-space-4` of gutter.
- Nothing is hidden at any width except decoration: the grid, the edge rails' labels (the ticks
  stay), and the citation's directory prefix.

## 8. Motion

**One clock, in one place** (formula §1 rule 4): `--at-dur-*` and `--at-ease-*` in the token file
are the entire motion vocabulary, and no JavaScript in this app types a millisecond — every JS
duration is read back out of the cascade with the kit's `useTokens` / `cssMs` / `secs`.

| Token | Budget | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `--at-dur-hair` | the shortest thing on the surface | rule weight and ground on hover/focus |
| `--at-dur-ink` | text in and out | the second beat of every staged move |
| `--at-dur-lens` | the lens spreading down the stack | vermilion arriving on the bands |
| `--at-dur-move` | **the level change, ≤ 400ms per the rubric** | L0↔L1, L1↔L2 |

The rules this surface is built to obey, and where:

- **Rule 1 — the level you leave carries the camera.** The outgoing level renders once more as an
  inert, id-free echo (`.at-echo`) with the whole scale-and-fade on it; the arriving level mounts
  at its final size so a shared-element morph is never measured inside an animating ancestor.
- **Rule 2 — one claimant per shared id.** The band owns `band-<layer>` until the L1 head takes
  it; the component row owns `part-<id>` until the pane takes it. Both through the kit's
  `sharedIdentity` / `useSharedIdentity`, so the key flips in the same commit.
- **Rule 3 — box, then ink.** Both directions, and it is why `--at-dur-ink` exists as its own
  token: the band's box travels first and the L1 column heads and rows fade in after it has
  landed; on the way back the ink leaves before the box travels. The L2 pane grows from its row
  empty and fills after.
- **Rule 6 — abortable.** Nothing is ever disabled while a move plays; Escape mid-flight lands
  back where the reader was standing (`useLevelFlight` → `nav.setMoving` → `escapeAbortsFlight`).
- **Rule 7 — presence from the model.** `presenceOf()` is the only thing that decides what
  recedes. Atlas never re-derives an opacity.
- **Rule 8 — reduced motion lands on the final state at frame zero.** Not a faster animation: the
  duration tokens go to `0ms` under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the echo is not rendered at
  all, and the lens simply *is* vermilion on the frame it is set.
- **Rule 9 — what is not seen costs nothing.** There is no renderer here to idle: no canvas, no
  three.js, no `requestAnimationFrame` loop anywhere in the app. A settled Atlas does zero work.

**Banned motion:** springs (a spring has no budget, so a level change cannot use one), any idle or
looping animation, opacity pulses, skeleton shimmer, parallax, and animating anything but
`transform`, `opacity` and colour.

## 9. Banned

- `Inter`, and the default serif stacks (`Times`, `Georgia`, `Garamond`).
- Shadows of any kind. `--at-elev-0: none` is the only elevation token and it is `none`.
- Rounded corners. `--at-radius-0: 0px` is the only radius token.
- A second accent. One hue, one meaning.
- Purple/blue "AI" glows, neon gradients, and any chroma not bound to a named finding.
- Raw `px` or `ms` outside `style/base/tokens.css` — enforced by `design/check-tokens.mjs`, which
  runs as part of `pnpm --filter atlas lint`.
- Uppercase micro-labels.
- Emoji, anywhere.
- A node-link hairball drawn over the plate (§5).
- Any runtime fetch: no network, no font request, no database, no telemetry. The model is a
  TypeScript module under `data/` and it is compiled into the bundle.
