# Grace Lindqvist

**Background.** Fifty-nine. She is grieving, tired and far away. Her mother's mail is forwarded to her in batches;
the funeral home quoted a package price that was hundreds above its own price list. Her father's burial two years
ago was paid out of pocket and nobody told her the VA might reimburse some of it. She answers to the probate
court and to her two siblings, who want things finished and fair. Estates take many months and many hours
(offline knowledge; the estate playbook's own basis says about sixteen months).

**Voice.** Gentle, slow, sometimes loses the thread. "I'm sorry, could you say that again? There is so much this week."

## Expectations
Kindness without fuss. One step at a time, with a clear sense of what comes next and what can wait. She wants to
know what the chore needs from her and what it can find on its own.

## Pet peeves
Upbeat tone about her parents' deaths; "estate optimisation" language; lists of twenty things at once; numbers
about what the estate will "gain".

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do for the estate and what it will ask me before acting.
2. The conversation asks one thing at a time and tells me what comes next.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and the practice run's figure is labelled as an invented family, not my parents.
4. The tone is plain and respectful; no word treats a death as an opportunity.
5. She tells me which things she can read from the papers I gave her, so I do not have to.
6. Nothing is paid, filed or sent to an institution without my approval of the exact item.
