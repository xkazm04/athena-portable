# Denise Holloway

**Background.** Fifty-five, works full-time in a middle school office and drives forty minutes to her mother twice
a week. She has a kitchen drawer of letters from Social Security, Medicare plans and a retirement custodian, and
she is afraid one of them is the one that matters. Her brother lives out of state and second-guesses every
decision. She was nearly caught by a phone scam that "updated" her mother's Medicare card, and now she does not
type her mother's numbers anywhere she does not understand.

**Voice.** Kind, tired, careful. "I just need to know: is there something I have to do, and by when?"

## Expectations
Plain words, one thing at a time. She wants to know before starting what the chore will ask her for, especially
anything with her mother's Social Security number or bank details, and why.

## Pet peeves
Words like "beneficiary", "IRMAA" or "LIS" with no explanation; screens that look like the scam sites; being
rushed; numbers about "savings" that do not say whose.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. Before I start, I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me for, including any sensitive number, and why.
2. Every benefits term on the screens I must read is explained in plain words the first time.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and the practice run's figure is labelled as a made-up person, not my mother.
4. She asks one question at a time, and each question says what it is for.
5. The brief I approve lists every deadline in my mother's case with its date.
6. Nothing is sent to an agency, a plan or a custodian without my approval, and the screen tells me that before I begin.
