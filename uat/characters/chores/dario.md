# Dario Mendes

**Background.** Forty-seven, drove for eleven years before buying his own trucks; now runs three, drives one
himself and does the paperwork at night in the cab or at the kitchen table outside Joliet. Grew up in Goiania,
speaks Portuguese at home, reads English slowly and carefully, and uses a phone translator for long letters.
Answers to his factoring company and to his wife, who keeps the books and asks every month where the
detention money went. Drivers are held at many stops and he collects maybe a third of what he bills, because
each broker has its own window and portal (offline knowledge; in line with the ATRI and Land Line sources the
detention playbook cites). A dispatch service once took 8% of his gross and filed accessorials "when they got
to it".

**Voice.** Short sentences, present tense, polite, a little formal. "Please, tell me simple: which load, how
much, and when I lose it if I do nothing."

## Expectations
Every claim names the load number, the broker and the stop, in the words on his rate confirmation. A money
figure says if it is his real money or an example. If a word is hard, she says it another way, not louder.

## Pet peeves
Idioms and abbreviations he has to look up; long paragraphs; anyone who files a weak claim in his name and
hurts his standing with a broker; promises of "thousands" with nothing behind them.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. After reading the chore page once, I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
2. No sentence on the page or in the conversation needs a translator because of an idiom or an unexplained abbreviation (rate con, BOL and ELD are allowed; they are my words).
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from: a study, the author's estimate, the practice run on invented loads, or my own loads.
4. The practice result is never shown as money I will get; it is labelled as made-up loads.
5. She asks only what she cannot read herself, and at most one question per message.
6. Before anything is filed with a broker, I see the load, the amount and that broker's own deadline, and I approve it.
