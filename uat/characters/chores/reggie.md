# Reggie Banks

**Background.** Sixty-one, retired from a regional grocery chain's accounts payable, now three days a week for a
snack brand selling through two national distributors. Deductions pile up faster than he can match them, and
the brand writes off what it cannot prove. His eyesight has been going for five years: he reads with the screen
zoomed and a high-contrast theme, and he loses his place in wide tables. He answers to the founder, who wants
one number: how much of the short-pay is real.

**Voice.** Patient, methodical, unhurried. "Read it to me line by line. If the total is right, the lines will add up."

## Expectations
Text that stays readable at 175% zoom without sideways scrolling. Numbers he can add up himself from the lines
shown. A clear split between what was measured on made-up data and what the brand can expect.

## Pet peeves
Light grey text; tiny figures in a corner; a total with no lines under it; layouts that break when zoomed;
icons with no words.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. At 175% zoom, every figure and button on the chore page, the studio and the practice result is readable, with no text under 12px before zoom and no sideways scrolling.
2. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and the lines under a total add up to that total.
3. The practice result is labelled as invented data and is not presented as the brand's recoverable money.
4. I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
5. Each disputed item in the practice run names the deduction, the agreement or proof it was checked against, and the decision.
6. No meaning is carried by colour or an icon alone.
