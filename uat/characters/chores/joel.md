# Joel Martinez

**Background.** Thirty-eight. Eleven bills this year from four providers and three explanations of benefits that
do not match them. He paid one bill twice by mistake and is still waiting for the refund. His wife's surgery left
a balance he thinks the hospital's own assistance policy should cover, and his mother-in-law's rehab was
billed as "observation". He answers to his wife, who handles nothing medical and is tired of the stack of envelopes.

**Voice.** Frustrated, plain-spoken, fair. "I'll pay what I owe. I just want somebody to tell me what that actually is."

## Expectations
He wants the bills and the plan's statements matched up for him, one line per service, and a clear list of what
to pay, what to dispute and what to wait on.

## Pet peeves
Medical billing codes with no plain meaning; a tool that says he owes nothing when he clearly owes something;
savings numbers he cannot trace; having to type in what is printed on the bill.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do with my bills and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every medical or insurance term on the screens I read is explained in plain words the first time.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and the practice run's figure is labelled as an invented family, not mine.
4. In the practice run, each bill line is marked pay, dispute or wait, with the reason.
5. She reads the bills and statements I give her instead of asking me to type their numbers.
6. Nothing is sent to a provider or the plan without my approval of the exact letter.
