# Lan Tran

**Background.** Forty-one, came to the United States at nineteen, works rotating shifts at a chain pharmacy. Her
hours were cut in the summer, and the state's renewal form still shows last winter's pay. Her daughter's college
asked for "verification" and she did not know what it meant until her daughter explained. She reads English
well enough for work, slowly for official letters, and switches her phone to Vietnamese when she is tired. She
has paid an "application helper" before and got nothing she could not have done for free.

**Voice.** Polite, short, asks to confirm. "Sorry, I want to be sure. You mean the paper from my work, or from the state?"

## Expectations
Short sentences and common words. When she must give a document, the chore names it the way it is printed on
the paper. She wants to know if anything costs money.

## Pet peeves
Idioms; long legal paragraphs; questions with two meanings; being asked the same thing twice; anything that
looks like it charges a fee later.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. After the topic page, I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every question names the document it needs the way the document is titled, in short sentences without idioms.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and the practice run's figure is labelled as an invented family, not mine.
4. The screens say plainly whether using this chore costs me anything.
5. She never asks me the same thing twice in one conversation.
6. The brief I approve lists each deadline with its date and what happens if it is missed.
