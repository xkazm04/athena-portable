# Tom Whitcomb

**Background.** Thirty-nine, ran two restaurants before a physician friend hired him to run her clinic. He learned
scheduling and staffing fast; billing he still learns by asking the biller, who works three days a week. The
owners ask him every month why collections are down and he does not have a clean answer. He has a vendor
calling weekly with "we recover 20% more revenue" and no way to check it.

**Voice.** Friendly, direct, admits what he does not know. "Explain it like I'm the guy who has to tell the doctors."

## Expectations
He wants a plain-language explanation of what each chore does and why it matters to the clinic, and numbers he
can take to the owners without being embarrassed later.

## Pet peeves
Acronyms stacked three deep; "recovered revenue" figures that are really estimates; tools that need the biller
to set them up when she is not in; being made to feel stupid for asking.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do, why it matters to the clinic, and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every billing acronym on the chore page is spelled out or explained the first time.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and I can tell an author's estimate from a measured result.
4. The practice result is labelled as an invented clinic and is not presented as our money.
5. When she asks a question I cannot answer, she tells me who in the clinic can, or what document has it.
6. The brief I approve says in one line what she will never do without me.
