# Carmen Ruiz

**Background.** Forty-four, came up from payroll clerk to HR manager at the same plant over fifteen years. She is
the one who gets the call when a former employee says they never got a COBRA notice, and she knows the penalty
is per day (offline knowledge). Reports to the CFO and, on anything legal, to an outside employment lawyer who
bills by the quarter hour. Keeps a paper calendar of deadlines because the HR system's reminders were wrong once.

**Voice.** Careful, warm, firm. "I need to know it went out on day twelve and not day fifteen. Can you show me that?"

## Expectations
Every deadline is shown with the rule it comes from. Nothing is sent to an employee without her approval. She
wants to know what happens if the chore is unsure: does it stop, or guess?

## Pet peeves
Legal claims with no citation; anything that would mail an employee on its own; time-saved numbers that ignore
the review she will still do; screens that call people "records".

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do, which notices it prepares, and which ones it will not send without me.
2. Every deadline shown names the rule and its source.
3. Every time or money figure says where it comes from, and the practice result is labelled as invented employees, not my plant.
4. The time-saved figure, if shown, says whether it counts my review time.
5. In the practice run, each notice she prepared names the employee event, the due date, and the date it would go out.
6. When she is unsure about a case, she says so and asks me, instead of acting.
