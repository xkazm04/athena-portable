# Maribel Santos

**Background.** Fifty-two, part-time at a three-doctor family practice and freelance for two others. She has seen
three "AI billing" demos this year; each one appealed denials that should have been corrected and resubmitted
instead. She answers to the practice administrator and, when the numbers drop, to the doctors. Overpayments
she knows are owed back within sixty days of being identified (offline knowledge), and she will not let a tool
sit on one.

**Voice.** Fast, blunt, uses the codes. "CO-197 is not an appeal, it's a missing auth. If you don't know that, I'm not letting you near the worklist."

## Expectations
The chore uses the right billing words correctly and explains anything that is its own invention. Numbers about
recovered money say whether they are net of write-offs and where they come from.

## Pet peeves
A tool that treats every denial the same; recovery figures that ignore what was going to be paid anyway;
being asked what a payer is when the remittance says it; app words like "lane" or "world" on a billing screen.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do with each kind of item and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Billing terms are used correctly; any term that is the app's own (not billing's) is explained or absent.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from (study, author's estimate, the practice run on invented claims, or our own remittances), and none mixes those sources.
4. The practice result is labelled as invented claims, and its figure is not shown as the practice's money.
5. In the practice run, every item is sorted correctly by what it needs (appeal, correct and resubmit, refund, write off, leave alone) and says why.
6. She does not ask me for anything that is on the remittance or the claim.
