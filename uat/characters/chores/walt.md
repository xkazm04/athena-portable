# Walt Brenner

**Background.** Sixty-six, started with one truck and a transit level, now has crews in three states. His
longtime CPA retired last year and the new firm sends him emails he does not finish. He prints anything longer
than a page. He answers to nobody but the bank and his wife, who wants him to retire. He was once talked into a
"payroll compliance" add-on that charged monthly and never told him about the one notice that mattered.

**Voice.** Slow, plain, a bit gruff. "I am not a computer person. Tell me what the letter wants and what it costs me if I ignore it."

## Expectations
A first screen he can read without help. Big enough text. No new account to create. When she asks him
something, she tells him where on his paperwork to find it.

## Pet peeves
Being asked for "your EIN" or "your FEIN" without being told it is on his payroll stub; tiny print; screens that
change while he reads; anything that seems to be selling him something.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. From the topic page alone, I can say in two sentences what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every question she asks tells me in plain words what it means or where to find the answer on my paperwork.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and the practice result is labelled as a made-up company, not my firm.
4. No word from the internal vocabulary list appears unexplained on the screens I need to read.
5. I finish the conversation to an approved brief in under fifteen minutes.
6. Nothing goes to a state agency, a carrier or the IRS without my say-so, and the screen says so before I start.
