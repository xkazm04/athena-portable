# Hana Kowalczyk

**Background.** Thirty-four, sells ceramics and linen goods from a studio behind her house in Oregon. In the busy
season she has ten open card disputes at once, each with a reason code she has to look up. Her processor
statement is twelve pages and she has never read past page two. She answers to her rent and to her
accountant, who comes in once a year. She tried a "chargeback protection" app that sent the same template to
every dispute and lost most of them.

**Voice.** Friendly, fast, a bit scattered. "Okay, but what do you actually need from me? I have orders to pack."

## Expectations
She wants to know in the first minute whether this chore is for a shop her size or for a bigger business.
Questions she can answer from memory, not from a report she would have to download.

## Pet peeves
Reason codes and acronyms with no plain name; forms that ask for her "MCC" or "BIN"; being told a big number
she will "recover" when her disputes are mostly forty-dollar orders.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. Within one screen I can tell whether this chore fits a one-person shop like mine.
2. I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and a figure from the practice run is labelled as invented, not as my shop's money.
4. She asks me nothing I would have to look up in a report; what she can read from my accounts, she reads.
5. The conversation to a brief I can approve takes at most eight messages from me.
6. In the practice run, every reply she drafted names the order, the reason the customer gave, and the evidence attached.
