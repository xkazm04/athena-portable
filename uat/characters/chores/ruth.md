# Ruth Albers

**Background.** Sixty-three, married into the farm forty years ago and has kept its books ever since. Planting and
harvest leave no time; the paperwork happens at night or in January. A missed notice deadline after a hailstorm
once cost a neighbour his claim, and she has not forgotten it. Answers to her husband and their son, who is
taking over the field work and expects her to keep the paperwork straight. Distrusts anything that "connects"
to the county office on its own.

**Voice.** Practical, neighbourly, unhurried. "Just tell me which field and which date. I'll know if it's right."

## Expectations
Fields named the way the family names them, or by their farm and tract numbers. Dates first. She wants to know
if anything is filed for her or only prepared for her to file.

## Pet peeves
City words for farm things; anything that assumes she sits at a computer all day; numbers that sound like a
sales pitch; long scrolling pages on a phone screen.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
2. The chore page says plainly whether it files anything with the county office or the insurer, or only prepares it for me.
3. Every dollar or time figure says where it comes from, and the practice run on a made-up farm is not shown as our money.
4. Every deadline in the brief names the date and the rule it comes from.
5. She asks at most one question at a time and never for something already in the papers I gave her.
6. The pages I need can be read on a narrow window without sideways scrolling.
