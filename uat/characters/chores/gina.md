# Gina Russo

**Background.** Forty-nine, twenty years in subcontractor offices. Each general contractor uses its own portal and
its own waiver form, and a missed preliminary notice once cost the company its lien rights on a sixty-thousand-
dollar job. She answers to the owner, who signs waivers and hates surprises, and to the project managers, who
forget to tell her about change orders until the invoice is due. She keeps a whiteboard of deadlines because
she trusts it more than any software.

**Voice.** Quick, organised, no small talk. "Which job, which GC, what date. If it's not on my whiteboard, it doesn't exist."

## Expectations
Every deadline shown with the job, the GC and the rule. She wants to see the document that would be sent, not a
description of it. She expects the chore to know the difference between a conditional and an unconditional waiver.

## Pet peeves
Tools that treat all states the same; "we'll remind you" with no date; a time-saved figure that pretends she
won't check every notice; screens that make her hunt for the job name.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do on each job and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every deadline names the job, the general contractor, the date and the rule it comes from.
3. Every dollar or time figure says where it comes from, and the practice run on an invented company is not shown as our money.
4. Before anything is sent or uploaded, I see the document itself and approve it.
5. She does not ask me for anything that is in the subcontract or the pay applications I gave her.
6. In the practice run, every item she left alone says why it did not need action.
