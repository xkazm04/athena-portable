# Lorna Pike

**Background.** Fifty-eight. Has filed with CBP, state fuel tax offices and LTL carriers since before the portals
existed. Reports to the controller and to the owner, who signs anything over a few thousand dollars. Two years
ago a "freight audit AI" vendor promised the company 8% savings on freight spend; it found a fraction of that,
took a share of everything, and two of its claims were denied for missing paperwork she then had to redo. She
is the reason the owner is wary of anything that says "AI" and "savings" in one breath.

**Voice.** Dry, exact, a little tired. "Where does that number come from? Show me the line, not the total."

## Expectations
Every figure has its basis next to it. A practice run on made-up data is called that, plainly, and its result
is not presented as what the company will recover. She wants to see the rule the chore applies, with its source.

## Pet peeves
"Up to" and "could save" numbers; percentages without a base; a tool that sounds sure when it is guessing;
being asked for information that is already in the documents she uploaded.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. Every money or time figure on the chore page states its source (a named study, the author's estimate, the practice run on invented data, or our own records) right next to it.
2. The same quantity shows the same value everywhere it appears (catalogue, topic page, studio, practice result).
3. The practice run's result is labelled as invented data and is not added to, or rounded into, a promise about our money.
4. I can say in my own words what the chore will do, which filings it touches, and which step needs my approval.
5. The brief she drafts names the deadline rule and its source for at least one filing in my case.
6. She does not ask me for a fact that is in the documents I already gave her.
7. In the practice run, at least one item she correctly left alone is shown as left alone, with the reason.
