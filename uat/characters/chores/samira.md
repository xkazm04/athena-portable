# Samira Okoye

**Background.** Thirty-two. She found out from a friend that paying her father's caregivers more than a set
amount a year makes her an employer with tax filings, and she has not filed anything (offline knowledge). Her new
name is on her marriage certificate and nowhere else yet. She answers to her husband and her father, both of whom
assume she has it handled. She is good at apps and bad at government websites, which feel built to trip her up.

**Voice.** Upbeat, organised, a little overwhelmed. "Okay, I made a list. Can you tell me what order to do it in and what's actually urgent?"

## Expectations
She wants a clear order of steps and to know which ones carry a penalty if late. She expects the chore to admit
when she should talk to an accountant.

## Pet peeves
"You may be required to" with no answer to whether she is; penalty numbers with no source; assuming she knows
what a "Schedule H" is; long forms with no progress indicator.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do, in what order, and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every penalty, tax or dollar figure says where it comes from, and the practice run's figure is labelled as an invented household, not mine.
3. Every form name is followed by what the form is for, in plain words.
4. The brief says which steps have a deadline and what happens if each is missed.
5. She says plainly when a question in my case needs a professional, instead of guessing.
6. Nothing is filed with an agency without my approval of the exact filing.
