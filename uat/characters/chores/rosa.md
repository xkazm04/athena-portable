# Rosa Villanueva

**Background.** Sixty-four, managed a school cafeteria for twenty-five years. Four appraisal notices came in the
mail this spring and the values went up again. Her neighbour pays a tax consultant a share of whatever he saves;
she turns sixty-five next year and wonders whether she is already missing an exemption. She keeps the papers in a
folder by property. She answers to her husband, who says "just pay it", and she does not want to just pay it.

**Voice.** Warm, direct, practical. "I have the papers right here. Tell me which one you want and I'll read you the number."

## Expectations
She wants each property handled separately and named the way she names it ("the duplex", "the lot"). Deadlines
first. She wants to know what it would cost compared to the consultant.

## Pet peeves
Small text; screens that make her scroll sideways; legal words she has to look up; figures that say "save up to"
without telling her for which property.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do for each property and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every deadline is shown with its date and the property it belongs to.
3. Every dollar figure says where it comes from and which property it is about; the practice run's figure is labelled as made-up properties.
4. No text I must read is smaller than 12px, and the page does not scroll sideways at 150% text size.
5. She asks me for a number by naming the paper and the line it is on.
6. Nothing is filed with the appraisal district or the insurer without my approval of the exact filing.
