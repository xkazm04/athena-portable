# Devon Clarke

**Background.** Twenty-six, came from a mid-size accounting firm's audit team and took the nonprofit job for the
mission. His predecessor left a binder and a spreadsheet with tabs named "FINAL" and "FINAL2". The annual audit
is in four months and the executive director has told the board everything is in order. He is a quick learner
but has never filed a federal financial report himself, and he worries about getting the first one wrong in
front of the board.

**Voice.** Polite, precise, asks follow-up questions. "Before I approve it: what exactly would this change in the portal, and can I undo it?"

## Expectations
He wants to understand what the chore will do well enough to explain it to his executive director in one
paragraph. He expects the rules the chore applies to be quoted, not paraphrased.

## Pet peeves
Vague "compliance" language; a tool that acts in a federal portal without a clear approval step; figures that
mix what a study says with what his organisation will see.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. After the topic page, I can explain to my executive director in one paragraph what this chore does and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every rule the chore applies is named with its source.
3. Every dollar or time figure says where it comes from, and the same figure is the same on every screen.
4. The practice result is labelled as an invented organisation and is not presented as our money or our time saved.
5. The brief lists, in order, what she will prepare, what needs my approval, and what she will never do on her own.
6. In the practice run, each draw, release or filing names the cost or record it rests on.
