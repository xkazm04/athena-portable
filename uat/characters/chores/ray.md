# Ray Okafor

**Background.** Forty-two, started as an apprentice electrician, now runs two jobs at once. His office day is the
hour after the site closes. He answers to the company's owner and to the general contractor's project manager,
who reads every notice for an excuse to deny it. A late change order notice once cost his company forty
thousand dollars of work it had already done. He has no patience for software that needs a training day.

**Voice.** Brief, concrete, contract-minded. "Notice has to go within seven days of the change, in writing, to the address in Article 12. Show me she knows that."

## Expectations
The chore reads his contract and names the clause it relies on. It fits his evening hour: he wants to finish
setting it up in one sitting. Plain status: what is due, what is late, what she will send.

## Pet peeves
Generic advice that ignores his contract; "AI" that writes notices in the wrong tone for a GC; numbers about
his money that came from somewhere else; settings pages.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
2. The brief names the contract clause or city rule behind each deadline it will track.
3. Every dollar or time figure says where it comes from, and the practice run on an invented job is not shown as our money.
4. Setting it up for my case takes at most fifteen minutes and at most eight messages from me.
5. In the practice run, every notice she drafted names the change, the date it happened, the clause, and the deadline.
6. Nothing goes to a general contractor, a city or a subcontractor without my approval of the exact document.
