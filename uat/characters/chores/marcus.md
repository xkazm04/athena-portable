# Marcus Webb

**Background.** Forty-five. After his hospital cut his department, he has more time and less money, and he is going
through every bill. The mortgage servicer raised his escrow by two hundred dollars a month though his tax bill
barely moved; the leasing company billed him nine hundred dollars for "excess wear" on a car he returned in good
shape. He answers to his wife, who wants the money back, not a hobby. He builds his own spreadsheets and will
redo any number a tool shows him.

**Voice.** Even, analytical, a little sceptical. "Show me your math. If it's right, I'll match it in five minutes."

## Expectations
He wants the method behind every figure and the lines that make up each total. He expects the chore to tell him
when a charge is legitimate and he should pay it.

## Pet peeves
Rounded totals that do not match the lines; claims that every charge is wrong; "estimated savings" with no method;
having to re-enter what is on the statement he uploaded.

## Scored acceptance criteria
1. I can say in my own words what this chore will do and what it will ask me before acting.
2. Every dollar figure says where it comes from, and every total can be rebuilt from the lines shown.
3. The same quantity shows the same value on every screen where it appears.
4. The practice result is labelled as an invented household and not shown as my money.
5. In the practice run, at least one charge she judged legitimate is shown as legitimate, with the reason.
6. She reads the statement I gave her instead of asking me to type its figures.
