# Blue Hour

## Philosophy
Art direction: a cinematic blue-hour railway, lit only by its own lamps.

The bet is the flow: show what is moving and the one red signal that only a human hand clears. The landing is a film frame at dusk. The Muse Spark train halts at G3, a proposal card is pinned to the box, and a brass lever waits in the foreground. Six lineside plates open into drawn scenes, each with a detail level.

## How it stays readable at scale
- The landing is one 1600 × 900 stage, scaled whole, with its art bleeding past the edges. At 390 px it becomes a stacked page.
- The roof is an illuminated mimic panel: six brass-framed sections with two to four large aggregates each.
- Detail text appears only once it renders at 12 px or more.
- Places never move. Signing changes lamps, stamps and counts.
- Wayfinding: breadcrumb, L0/L1/L2 ladder, a key plan, `/` find, arrow keys, Escape, Home.

## The wow moment
Drag the lever down at dusk. The red bloom turns green, the card is stamped "signed, runs once" and the train leaves. On the roof, answer the three levers one by one. When none waits, 1A41 runs from G3 to the Ledgerbox platform.

## Known limits
- Everything is simulated on fixtures; Muse costs are estimates and SAM agreement is simulated.
- The Gauntlet's 93 turns and 0 breaches were measured on other models. The Muse re-run is shown as planned.
- The landing lever cycles through the fixture cards; only the roof records decisions.
- At 2560 px the detail tier shows at L0, and a few long labels are clipped.
- On a phone the roof is a list: levers first, then sections.
