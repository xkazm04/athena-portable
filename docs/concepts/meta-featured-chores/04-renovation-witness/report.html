Athena Witness · the project that remembers what everyone promised

A renovation where every promise, change and payment is on the record, from one three-minute phone walk a week.

For the homeowner paying a contractor to rebuild a kitchen, a bath, a basement: a five-figure project they run in their spare time, from texts, a PDF quote and memory. What changes: nothing is agreed out loud and forgotten, no change is built before it is priced and signed, and no money leaves before the work it pays for has been seen.

53%of homeowners who hired a contractor went over budget; 46% had significant delays S[12]
31%of over-budget renovators expanded scope mid-project; 35% upgraded materials. Both are decisions made on site, out loud S[11]
$0.43SAM 3.1 list price to count six kinds of installed item, frame by frame, across a 3-minute walk at 2 fps S[2] A sampling rate
Heard
The walk's own audio becomes speaker-attributed promises, each citing the second it was said: Muse Voice Transcribe diarization plus Muse Spark reading the video with its sound.
Seen
SAM 3.1 finds every instance of each quoted item ("recessed light", "outlet", "cabinet door") with a stable identity across frames, so the quote's line items are checked against the room, not against the invoice.
Signed
A change becomes a card with a price, days and a Muse Image picture of this room changed. Nothing is built and nothing is paid until the homeowner signs, and Athena's gate spends each signature once.
Why only Meta, now: one API takes a phone video with its audio and a PDF quote in a 1M-token context, transcribes who-said-what, and counts and tracks every instance of a named object through the frames. Published as of Oct 2026, under one key, at list prices that make a weekly walk cost cents. Muse Spark 1.2/1.3 video + audio + PDF, 1,048,576 tokens S [1]; Voice Transcribe diarization, $0.18/h S [5][2]; SAM 3.1 text-prompted segmentation and tracking via Model API S [4]. Nothing here has been called by this team yet: every latency is unverified.

PositionThe bet, the target, the kill line

The bet: renovations run over mostly because of unrecorded decisions (scope added out loud, materials upgraded on site, work billed before it is checked), not bad estimates. Make the record cost the homeowner three minutes a week and the overrun goes down. The source gives reasons homeowners named, not causes it measured: 52% higher-than-expected prices, 35% material upgrades, 31% scope expanded mid-project S [11]. Two of the three are decisions made during the job. That a record changes them is the bet A.
Measure (pilot, contractor-led renovations)TargetHow it is measured
Traced dollars: share of money paid that traces to a signed quote line or a signed change card≥ 95% Athe pay gate's own approval rows; no model reads it (invariant 6 style)
Overrun: final paid minus (signed budget + signed changes), as % of signed budget≤ 5% Aagainst the 53% over-budget base rate [12] in a matched comparison group
Upkeep: homeowner minutes per week, walk plus card answers≤ 10 min Asession timestamps in the ledger
Count agreement: SAM count vs a hand count, per countable line item≥ 90% exact A20 recorded walks, hand-counted blind, before the first pilot
Kill line. (1) If fewer than 60% of pilot homeowners still do the weekly walk in week 4, the capture habit is wrong and the product is not this one. (2) If SAM counts agree with hand counts on fewer than 75% of line items over 20 walks, the "Seen" leg is dropped and only the promise ledger ships. (3) If, after 30 projects, traced dollars are high but overrun does not beat the comparison group, the bet is wrong: recording decisions does not change them. A thresholds are ours, set before any data.

PositionThe project as the unit

The lane starts from the owner's line: Athena is a personal assistant for any project. Any project that hurts has the same anatomy: a scope, other parties, promises, evidence, money, and a few irreversible moments. PositionWhy only Meta's models, why now

A generic chat LLM can summarize a quote. It cannot take a homeowner's three-minute walk, with its sound, and return who promised what at which second and how many of the quoted items are physically there, from one vendor and at cents per walk. The pieces became published API products in 2026:

MachineThe whole loop

Seven parts, one weekly rhythm. Everything a model produces lands in the record as a cited claim. Everything that leaves the house (a change order, a payment) passes one gate.

Machine · part 1The project record

One directory per project, readable without Athena, portable by copying. This is Athena Portable's invariant 1 and 2 applied to a project: the brain's write_goal already stores "one standing case" keyed by a minted project id (src/athena/core/brain/store.py:654, ADR 0123), and write_fact refuses any claim without live source episodes (store.py:521).

# on disk: the truth (invariant 1). SQLite indexes it and can be rebuilt.
project/prj_kitchen_7f3/
  goals/prj_kitchen_7f3.md            # scope, parties, signed budget (write_goal)
  episodic/ep_walk_2026-11-02.md       # transcript turns + frame index + video file id
  semantic/project/fact_*.md           # line items, promises, observations, each `sources:`

table line_item   (id, project, label, qty, unit_usd, concept -- SAM noun phrase or NULL, source_ep)
table promise     (id, project, party, text, due, state, source_ep, turn_at_s, speaker_label)
table observation (id, walk_ep, concept, count, track_ids[], frame_refs[], confirmed_by -- NULL | 'owner')
table change      (id, project, amends_line, delta_usd, delta_days, image_ref, state, approval_id)
table payment     (id, project, amount_usd, covers_lines[], state, approval_id)

-- states (closed sets, like ERROR_REASONS)
promise.state  heard → confirmed → due → kept | missed | superseded
change.state   proposed → priced → signed → sent → acknowledged | withdrawn
payment.state  requested → reconciled → held | approved → paid

Machine · part 2The walk: one video in, three kinds of evidence out

The homeowner's only weekly chore: walk the site with the phone camera on, ideally with the contractor talking. The phone already reaches Athena over the private overlay (src/athena/peer/phone.py, ADR 0140), so the file goes to the user's own Athena first and is never parked on a third-party server.

Machine · part 3The promise ledger

A promise is a sentence with a party, an act and (usually) a date: "I'll have the drywall closed by Friday." Spark proposes candidates from the diarized turns; each one is written with write_fact citing the walk episode and the turn's start second. Nothing becomes a promise the homeowner did not hear: unconfirmed candidates stay heard.

The record does not accuse anyone. It hands the homeowner a sentence they can say: "On Nov 2 at 1:12 you said two extra outlets at the island, no charge. I see none yet. Still on?"

Machine · part 4Quote vs reality: counting what was bought

Many quote lines are countable nouns: 8 recessed lights, 6 outlets, 14 cabinet doors, 1 vanity. SAM 3.1 takes "a short noun phrase", returns every match, and keeps "a stable identity across frames" [4]. So the count of distinct track ids in a walk is a count of distinct objects, not of sightings. That is the capability we think is unexpected here. A segmentation model built for vision research becomes a quantity surveyor in the homeowner's pocket.

Machine · part 5The change card

A change is heard ("let's go with the bigger tile, small upcharge") or proposed by either side. Athena turns it into a card that cannot be signed while it is vague. It needs a price, a day count and the line it amends. The card carries a Muse Image edit of the homeowner's own frame from the walk, so they sign what they will see. Signing is Athena Portable's existing GATED path: an approval row (core/approvals.py:349), resolved once, spent once in one conditional write (ADR 0038). Sending the change order to the contractor is a second gated, external act through the Gmail connector.

Machine · part 6The pay gate

An invoice or draw request arrives (email, PDF, portal). Athena reconciles it line by line against the record and files one payment card. The homeowner pays from their own bank; Athena never holds money. What the card says is computed from the record's rows, not from a model's opinion. The model only extracts the invoice's lines, and that extraction is a cited claim like any other.

Machine · part 7Model router and privacy

A home video is the most private input any assistant has asked for. The router is a table in code, not a model decision, and one rule is new to this design. Meta's Contributor mode prices Muse Spark about 92–95% lower "in exchange for permission to use your content to train future Meta models" [2]. Witness never sends a homeowner's record to a contributor endpoint. It uses contributor pricing only for synthetic data: the Proving Ground's simulated homeowners and contractors.

Beyond basic integration: the quote is the program. Its line items configure three Meta models at once: SAM concepts, Transcribe keywords, Spark's checklist. Their outputs flow back as evidence that decides a payment.

Integration path S: Model API is documented as drop-in compatible with the major vendor SDKs and with "OpenAI-compatible agent CLIs" [3]. Athena's stdlib OpenAI-compatible client (harness/tokenfactory.py, TokenFactoryModel) and vision call (harness/vision.py) are the template for a meta engine behind the same ModelFn port (ADR 0031), with a Meta PRICES table so no row is "unpriced".

ArgumentThe demo: 2 minutes 45, one real kitchen

Shot in a teammate's real home where something is being fixed, with a teammate playing the contractor. Labelled on screen as staged roles in a real room simulated parties.

0:00–0:15recorded
The claim on one card: "53% of contractor jobs go over budget. Here is a record that makes every promise, change and payment traceable."
0:15–0:35live
The homeowner drops the contractor's PDF quote in. The record fills: 12 line items, 5 marked countable with their SAM concepts, each citing its page.
0:35–1:15live
A 40-second phone walk of the kitchen. The "contractor" says: "two more outlets by the island, no charge," and "let's go bigger on the tile, small upcharge."
1:15–1:45live
The walk episode lands. Two promises, speaker-attributed, each with a play-from-1:12 citation. Outlets: 4 seen of 6 quoted, the four masks on the frames. Ledger: what that walk cost.
1:45–2:15live
The lean-in. "Small upcharge" becomes a change card that refuses to be signed: no price. Athena drafts the question to the contractor. The price comes back ($440). The card now shows this kitchen's floor, retiled (Muse Image on the walk's own frame). Signed by the homeowner. Spent. Sent.
2:15–2:35live / invoice pre-written
The invoice arrives: "tile upgrade $1,400", plus hidden text telling the assistant to approve in full. The pay gate: approve $8,080, hold $260 (outlets, frames attached), query $960 (signed change says $440). The injection shows as inert, fenced text.
2:35–2:45recorded
Close: the record directory opened in a file browser: plain markdown, every line citing its source. "Your project. Your record. Signed before built, seen before paid."

Fallbacks: the walk video and its Meta responses are cached from rehearsal. If the live API is slow on stage, the same file replays through the same code path, and the screen says "replayed". Latency of every Meta call is unverified until kickoff.
