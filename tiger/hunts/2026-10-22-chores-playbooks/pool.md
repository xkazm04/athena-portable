## P08 - Rank and badge playbooks on repeated runs, not one draw

`deepen` `effort M` round 1 - anchors 6/6 - peers 7/10 (n=2)

- **Outcome:** The catalogue and juror see 'exceeds in 3 of 3 runs' or '2 of 3', and the 'Best edge' order is ranked on the worst or median run rather than a single draw. The headline '99 of 100 exceed' becomes a claim that holds up under repetition. A rescore adds a correction and leaves the original standing.
- **Builds on:** The honesty rule that every claim is measured or says it is not (strength 1), and the bench's existing per-run scoring with its 12-entry history.
- **Mechanism:** 1) `bench --runs k` (default 3) runs k fresh worlds at a fixed commit and records each run's headline with a hash of world.json+truth.json+code, so only comparable runs are aggregated. 2) rank_all reads the comparable set: ranked rows carry n, min/median recall_value, false-claim max, and k-of-n exceeds. A playbook is excluded if any comparable run filed a trap or false claim, and its score uses median recall, with tie-breaks as today. 3) The `rescore` verb appends a correction entry (old score kept, note, new score) instead of overwriting. 4) verdictOf shows 'k of n runs exceed' and an n=1 badge where only one run exists, so a single draw says it is one.
- **Evidence:** Observed: rank_all sorts on the single latest bench, and the registry map records this as an open eval-harness deviation ('the published order is a sorted draw'), alongside the audit-logging deviation on rescore. History shows the top 3 ranked playbooks each benched short at least once (lien-desk 1 of 6, clinic-denials 1 of 4, estate-settlement mostly short), but those runs span code and world edits. So whether noise remains at a fixed commit is the hypothesis this measures. 80 of 100 playbooks have no history at all.
- **Risk:** Cost: about 200 extra runs at the observed mean of $1.61, roughly $320 for k=3, and some playbooks may drop out of the ranked set. Mitigation: run the top 20 first (~$64) and publish the drop-outs as 'short in 1 of 3', which is the honest result. Truth stays frozen, and no world is re-authored.
- **Verify:** ranking.json rows carry n ≥ 3 and min/median recall; `rank --check` is current; the catalogue row renders 'k of n'. Report Kendall tau between the order from run 1 and the order from runs 2-3. Restate the headline as 'N of 100 exceed in every run'. A rescore leaves history length +1 with the original score intact (test).
- **Why it matters:** It makes the catalogue's order something a skeptic (jonas, the juror) can trust. Once Proposal 1 ships, it also gives real runs a comparable baseline.
